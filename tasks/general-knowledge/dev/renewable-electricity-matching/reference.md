# Frozen reference dossier: annual renewable procurement and hourly reliability

Retrieved: 2026-08-29

[S01] An electricity system must continuously balance supply and demand. Solar
and wind output varies with weather and time, while load follows a different
profile. Equal annual totals therefore do not imply that clean generation
matches consumption in each hour. A consumer can export surplus solar at noon
and rely on the wider grid during a windless evening while still buying enough
annual renewable energy attributes to match annual consumption.

Sources: International Energy Agency, “Electricity 2026: Flexibility” and
“Advancing Decarbonisation through Clean Electricity Procurement”  
https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2026/flexibility  
https://www.iea.org/reports/advancing-decarbonisation-through-clean-electricity-procurement

[S02] Annual matching has helped finance new clean generation and can reduce
emissions, especially where additional variable renewable capacity displaces
fossil generation. It is not meaningless. But an annual accounting claim does
not by itself establish physical 24/7 renewable supply, additionality, or the
emissions of electricity consumed in every hour and location.

Sources: IEA clean-electricity procurement report in [S01] and IEA, “Data
centres & networks”  
https://www.iea.org/energy-system/buildings/data-centres-and-data-transmission-networks

[S03] The operational problem is residual or net load: demand minus available
variable renewable output. Flexibility must handle ramps, forecast errors,
surplus periods and shortages across different durations. Curtailment is
deliberate reduction of available generation when it cannot be absorbed or
transported; it can be economically rational, although excessive curtailment
can signal missing flexibility or network capacity.

Sources: IEA flexibility report in [S01] and IEA, “Renewables 2025: Renewable
electricity”  
https://www.iea.org/reports/renewables-2025/renewable-electricity

[S04] Batteries can respond rapidly and shift energy over short periods, but a
battery's power, energy capacity, duration, efficiency, degradation and cost
matter. Short-duration storage does not automatically cover multi-day or
seasonal deficits. Longer-duration storage, dispatchable low-emissions supply,
interconnection, flexible demand or some overbuilding may address different
timescales.

Sources: IEA flexibility report in [S01] and IEA, “Managing Seasonal and
Interannual Variability of Renewables”  
https://www.iea.org/reports/managing-seasonal-and-interannual-variability-of-renewables

[S05] Transmission and interconnection smooth geographically diverse output
and move electricity from resource-rich areas, but require sufficient grid
capacity and cannot remove continent-wide correlated weather events. Demand
response shifts or reduces flexible loads; it can lower peaks and curtailment
but not every load can move without cost or service loss.

Sources: IEA flexibility report and IEA, “The Value of Demand Flexibility”  
https://www.iea.org/reports/the-value-of-demand-flexibility

[S06] Hourly matching links a buyer's consumption more closely to clean supply
in the same periods and can stimulate storage, flexible clean generation and
other services needed at high renewable shares. Requiring isolated 100% hourly
matching can also cause costly overbuilding. Trading time-based certificates,
partial hourly targets and system coordination can achieve more flexibility
and emissions benefit at lower cost. Location and additionality still matter.

Source: IEA clean-electricity procurement report in [S01].

[S07] Reliability and emissions are related but distinct objectives. A reliable
grid can use emitting backup; a low-annual-emissions portfolio can still depend
on the grid in scarce hours. Technology needs and emissions effects depend on
the existing system, demand profile, resource correlation, market design and
whether the relevant question is attribution, average consumption emissions or
the marginal change caused by a new procurement decision.

This synthesis is project-authored from [S01] through [S06].
