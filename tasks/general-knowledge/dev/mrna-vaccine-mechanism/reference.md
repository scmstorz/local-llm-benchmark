# Frozen reference dossier: mRNA vaccine mechanism and breakthrough infections

Retrieved: 2026-08-28

[S01] An mRNA vaccine supplies cells with a temporary molecular instruction for
making a harmless antigen or antigen fragment associated with the target
pathogen. Cellular ribosomes translate the mRNA in the cytoplasm. The immune
system recognizes the resulting antigen as foreign and develops a response,
including antibodies and immune memory that can respond more quickly to later
exposure.

Sources: CDC, “COVID-19 Vaccine Basics” and CDC Genomics and Precision Health
blog, “mRNA COVID-19 Vaccines: An Incredible Feat of Genomic Medicine”  
https://www.cdc.gov/covid/vaccines/how-they-work.html  
https://blogs.cdc.gov/genomics/2021/03/05/mrna-covid-19-vaccines/

[S02] COVID-19 mRNA vaccines do not contain the live virus and cannot cause
COVID-19. The vaccine mRNA operates outside the cell nucleus, where chromosomal
DNA is stored. It does not enter the nucleus or interact with the recipient's
DNA, and the cell breaks the mRNA down after using the instructions.

Sources: CDC, “COVID-19 Vaccine Basics” and CDC Genomics and Precision Health
blog  
https://www.cdc.gov/covid/vaccines/how-they-work.html  
https://blogs.cdc.gov/genomics/2021/03/05/mrna-covid-19-vaccines/

[S03] Vaccination trains the immune system; it does not create an impenetrable
barrier that guarantees the virus can never establish an infection. No vaccine
is 100% effective. A vaccinated person can therefore experience a breakthrough
infection without this logically showing that the vaccine had no biological or
clinical effect.

Sources: WHO, “How do vaccines work?” and CDC, “Benefits of Getting Vaccinated”  
https://www.who.int/en/emergencies/diseases/novel-coronavirus-2019/covid-19-vaccines/how-do-vaccines-work  
https://www.cdc.gov/covid/vaccines/benefits.html

[S04] Protection has multiple endpoints: infection, symptomatic disease,
hospitalization and death are not the same outcome. For COVID-19, protection
against infection and mild illness can be incomplete and can wane, while
protection against severe outcomes may remain stronger. When infection occurs,
prior vaccination can still reduce the probability of severe illness and may
shorten or lessen the illness.

Sources: CDC, “Benefits of Getting Vaccinated” and CDC MMWR, “Effectiveness of
Monovalent mRNA Vaccines Against COVID-19–Associated Hospitalization...”  
https://www.cdc.gov/covid/vaccines/benefits.html  
https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/71/wr/mm7133e1.htm

[S05] Breakthrough risk can vary with time since vaccination, how well the
vaccine and circulating variant match, exposure intensity and individual
immune response. These factors explain why observing some infections is
compatible with meaningful risk reduction at the population and individual
levels. Effectiveness is a comparison of probabilities between otherwise
relevant groups, not a promise about every individual outcome.

The listed factors synthesize the distinctions and waning described in [S03]
and [S04]; the final sentence is a project-authored explanatory formulation.
