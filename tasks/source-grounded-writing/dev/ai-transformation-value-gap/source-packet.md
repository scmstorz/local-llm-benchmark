# Source packet: AI transformation and the enterprise value gap

This packet deliberately does not form a continuous argument. It contains five
source-separated evidence cards. Each card preserves the scope, method,
measurements and limitations of its own source. Section order does not imply
importance, and no cross-source conclusion is supplied.

The stable labels `[S01]` through `[S25]` exist for evaluation traceability.
They must not appear in the candidate output.

## Source 1 — McKinsey Global Survey on AI

**Publication:** *The state of AI: How organizations are rewiring to capture
value*, McKinsey & Company, March 12, 2025.

**Source type:** Cross-sectional global survey with self-reported adoption and
business-impact measures.

[S01] The online survey ran from July 16 through July 31, 2024 and included
1,491 respondents in 101 countries. Respondents represented different regions,
industries, company sizes, functions and seniority levels. Results were weighted
by each respondent country's contribution to global GDP. Forty-two percent of
respondents worked for organizations with at least USD 500 million in annual
revenue.

[S02] Seventy-eight percent of respondents said their organization used AI in
at least one business function, compared with 72 percent in early 2024 and 55
percent one year earlier. Seventy-one percent reported regular generative-AI use
in at least one function, up from 65 percent in early 2024. Organizations used
AI in an average of three functions. The survey deliberately left "adopted"
undefined: the category can cover anything from limited experimentation by a
few employees to deeply embedded, redesigned business processes.

[S03] Reported value was much stronger at the level of individual business
units than at the enterprise level. More than 80 percent of respondents said
their organizations were not seeing tangible enterprise-level EBIT impact from
generative AI. Seventeen percent attributed at least 5 percent of EBIT over the
previous 12 months to generative AI. The report therefore describes rapidly
rising use alongside limited organization-wide bottom-line impact.

[S04] McKinsey compared 25 organizational attributes with respondents'
self-reported EBIT impact. Fundamental workflow redesign had the largest
relative association with EBIT impact across organizations of all sizes;
21 percent of respondents at organizations using generative AI said at least
some workflows had been fundamentally redesigned. CEO oversight of AI
governance was also associated with higher reported impact, especially in large
organizations. The reported correlation model had an R-squared of 0.20, so the
analysis does not establish that workflow redesign or CEO oversight caused the
reported financial outcomes.

[S05] The survey asked about 12 adoption and scaling practices, including
workflow integration, role-specific training, feedback mechanisms, road maps,
change communication, trust measures and KPI tracking. Fewer than one-third of
respondents said their organization followed most practices; fewer than one in
five said it tracked well-defined KPIs for generative-AI solutions. Respondents
most often said time saved through automation was used for new activities or
for existing responsibilities that had not been automated. The evidence is
self-reported and cross-sectional, not an experiment or audited financial
analysis.

## Source 2 — Generative AI at Work

**Publication:** Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li and Lindsey R. Raymond,
*Generative AI at Work*, NBER Working Paper 31161, revised November 2023;
published in *The Quarterly Journal of Economics* in 2025.

**Source type:** Staggered workplace deployment studied with operational data.

[S06] The researchers studied an AI conversational assistant deployed to 5,179
customer-support agents at a Fortune 500 business-software company and its
outsourcing partners. Their dataset covered about three million customer chats;
89 percent of agents were outside the United States, primarily in the
Philippines. The assistant monitored chats and suggested responses in real time.
Agents remained responsible for the conversation and could ignore its advice.
The primary productivity measure was successfully resolved issues per hour.

[S07] Access to the assistant increased resolved issues per hour by 14 percent
on average. The increase combined shorter handling time, more chats handled per
hour and a small increase in the share of chats successfully resolved. The
average is not a uniform treatment effect across the workforce.

[S08] Novice and lower-skilled workers gained much more: their resolved issues
per hour rose by 34 percent. Treated agents with two months of tenure performed
about as well as untreated agents with more than six months of tenure.
Experienced and highly skilled agents saw minimal productivity effects; the
study also found evidence that assistance could reduce conversation quality for
the most skilled agents. Generative AI therefore compressed performance
differences in this setting rather than simply raising every worker by the same
amount.

[S09] Several findings are consistent with the assistant transferring practices
associated with stronger agents. Workers who followed recommendations more
closely gained more; lower-skilled agents' language became more similar to that
of higher-skilled colleagues; and previously assisted workers performed above
their pre-AI baseline during unexpected tool outages. The authors call this
evidence suggestive rather than definitive. They also observed improved
customer sentiment, fewer requests to escalate to a supervisor and lower
attrition, particularly among newer workers.

[S10] The study does not measure aggregate employment, wages or long-run labor
demand. Increased productivity could ultimately reduce demand, expand demand or
change job responsibilities. Results may not generalize from a relatively
stable technical-support environment to rapidly changing work. The paper also
raises a distributional question: high performers supplied many examples from
which the system learned, received comparatively little direct productivity
benefit and were not separately compensated for that training contribution.

## Source 3 — Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier

**Publication:** Fabrizio Dell'Acqua et al., *Navigating the Jagged
Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of
Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality*,
*Organization Science*, published online March 11, 2026.

**Source type:** Preregistered randomized experiment with professional
knowledge workers.

[S11] The experiment involved 758 Boston Consulting Group consultants. After a
baseline assessment, participants were randomly assigned to no AI, GPT-4, or
GPT-4 plus a prompt-engineering overview. One experimental arm covered 18
realistic consulting subtasks designed to fall inside GPT-4's capability
frontier. A separate complex managerial case was deliberately selected to sit
outside that frontier. The tasks were developed with industry experts to
resemble professional consulting work.

[S12] On the 18 inside-frontier tasks, AI users completed 12.2 percent more
tasks and worked 25.1 percent faster on average. Their work was also rated
substantially higher in quality; the final paper summarizes the performance
improvement as more than 30 percent. Benefits appeared across creative,
analytical, writing and persuasion work rather than only basic drafting.

[S13] Lower performers on the baseline assessment gained more than higher
performers, although both groups benefited on inside-frontier tasks. This
equalizing result is conditional on tasks for which the model was capable. It
does not imply that less-experienced workers can safely delegate every task or
that expertise no longer matters.

[S14] On the deliberately outside-frontier managerial problem, participants
using AI were 19 percentage points less likely to reach the correct solution
than participants without AI. Tasks that seem comparably difficult to people
can lie on different sides of an AI system's capability boundary. Strong
performance on adjacent tasks may encourage misplaced reliance when the model's
answer is persuasive but wrong.

[S15] The paper's negative result rests on one selected outside-frontier task,
which the authors identify as an inherent limitation. Inside-frontier quality
was judged with subjective professional rubrics, whereas the outside task had
an objectively correct recommendation. Results apply most directly to
professional settings that prioritize quality within an allotted period;
effects may differ under other incentives. The frontier also moves as models
change, so organizations must evaluate concrete tasks rather than infer a
permanent boundary from one model generation.

## Source 4 — The Cybernetic Teammate

**Publication:** Fabrizio Dell'Acqua et al., *The Cybernetic Teammate: A Field
Experiment on Generative AI Reshaping Teamwork and Expertise*, NBER Working
Paper 33641, April 2025; published in *Organization Science* in 2026.

**Source type:** Preregistered randomized field experiment inside one company.

[S16] The study involved 776 experienced Procter & Gamble professionals working
on real early-stage product-innovation challenges. Participants were randomly
assigned to one of four conditions: an individual without AI, a two-person
cross-functional team without AI, an individual with AI, or a two-person team
with AI. Human teams paired one commercial and one R&D professional. The study
measured solution performance, movement across functional expertise boundaries
and participants' self-reported emotional experience.

[S17] Relative to individuals working without AI, human teams without AI
improved solution quality by 0.24 standard deviations. Individuals using AI
improved it by 0.37 standard deviations and teams using AI by 0.39 standard
deviations. Individuals with AI therefore reached a quality level comparable to
human teams without AI. Teams with AI did not significantly outperform
individuals with AI, suggesting that the immediate measured gain came mainly
from strengthening individual work rather than transforming human-to-human
collaboration.

[S18] AI also reduced time spent: individuals with AI used 16.4 percent less
time than the individual control group, while teams with AI used 12.7 percent
less. AI-assisted participants produced substantially longer solutions. The
quality finding is therefore not merely a speed result, but the experiment does
not show how these outputs performed through later implementation stages or in
the market.

[S19] Without AI, commercial professionals tended toward commercially oriented
ideas and R&D professionals toward technical ones. With AI, that distinction
largely disappeared and both groups proposed a more balanced mix without a
measured quality penalty. Employees for whom product development was not a core
task also improved strongly. AI users reported more positive emotions and less
anxiety or frustration than the no-AI individual control, but these are
self-reported responses during the experiment rather than long-term measures of
workplace well-being.

[S20] The experiment used one-day virtual collaborations following one firm's
early product-development routine. It did not reproduce extended coordination,
implementation, rework or larger and more complex team structures. Participants
were relatively inexperienced with prompting, and the AI tool was not designed
specifically for group collaboration. Those conditions could suppress benefits,
but they also limit generalization; they do not establish that AI can replace
the full social, organizational or execution role of human teams.

## Source 5 — Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI

**Publication:** Eleanor W. Dillon, Sonia Jaffe, Nicole Immorlica and
Christopher T. Stanton, *Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI*, NBER
Working Paper 33795, May 2025, revised November 2025.

**Source type:** Six-month randomized field experiment across multiple firms,
using workplace application telemetry.

[S21] Microsoft partnered with 66 large firms in a randomized experiment
covering 7,137 knowledge workers. Treated workers received Microsoft 365
Copilot integrated into email, meetings and document applications; control
workers continued with their existing tools. Firms were asked to preserve the
assignment for six months. Participants could choose whether and how much to
use the tool, and both treated and control workers could also have used other
generative-AI products.

[S22] More than 90 percent of treated workers used Copilot at least once, but
use peaked in the first five weeks. After 12 weeks, average weekly use
stabilized at just under 40 percent of treated workers. Firm-level differences
explained the largest share of variation in use, more than broad observable
categories such as industry. The authors point to unmeasured firm-specific
management or training as plausible contributors, without claiming a causal
mechanism.

[S23] In months four through six, assignment to Copilot reduced Outlook email
session time by an estimated 1.4 hours per week, or 12 percent of the pre-period
mean. An instrumental-variable estimate for workers who used Copilot was two
hours, or 17 percent. Users also gained about 1.5 hours of longer email-free
blocks during the workday and reduced work in measured digital applications
outside regular hours by roughly 0.25 hours per week, or 9 percent.

[S24] Treated workers replied to about the same number of email threads,
attended the same number of Teams meetings and completed the same number of
Word documents as controls. The researchers did not detect meaningful changes
in task quantity or composition. They could not observe email or document
content and had no direct productivity or performance-quality measure, so less
application time must not be reported as a measured increase in business output
or quality.

[S25] The experiment found individual time savings but no evidence that an
individual-level tool rollout reorganized work processes. Close coworkers'
access did not yield precise evidence of team-level restructuring, but even the
study's higher-saturation teams had only 39 percent of coworkers equipped on
average. The authors frame the study as an early-adoption snapshot and call for
further research on complementary process innovation, team-level deployment
and firm-level transformation.
